01 · The Problem

A café with great coffee,
invisible online

Brew & Bloom is a charming neighbourhood café with loyal customers — but zero digital presence. Customers had no way to browse the menu, check prices, or place orders without walking in. The challenge was to design their first-ever digital experience from scratch.

"How do you design for everyone — from a tech-savvy student to a grandparent ordering their first coffee online?"

— The central design question
01

No Online Presence

The café existed only physically. Potential customers searching online couldn't find the menu, hours, or any way to order — meaning lost business every day.

02

All-Age Accessibility

The customer base ranged from college students to older adults. Any solution needed to work for first-time smartphone users as much as digital natives.

03

Warmth Must Translate

The café's biggest asset is its cosy, welcoming atmosphere. The digital experience had to feel equally warm — not clinical or generic like typical food delivery apps.

04

Simple Ordering Flow

The ordering journey had to be intuitive enough to complete in under 2 minutes — minimal steps, clear hierarchy, no confusion about what to tap next.

02 · Design Process

From blank canvas
to live website

Following the Google UX Design framework — a structured, user-centred approach even for a solo course project.

1
Empathise
Identified target users across age groups. Considered varying levels of digital literacy and café ordering contexts (dine-in vs. takeaway).
2
Define
Defined the core problem: users need a simple, warm digital space to browse and order from a café with no existing online presence.
3
Ideate
Sketched multiple layout approaches. Prioritised card-based menus, large tap targets, and a minimal checkout flow for accessibility.
4
Prototype
Built low-fidelity wireframes in Figma (Google UX Certificate project), then high-fidelity designs and live site in Canva.
03 · Design Decisions

Every choice had
a reason

Good UX isn't just about how it looks — it's about why every element is where it is.

🎨

Warm Colour Palette

Creams, browns, and blush tones were chosen to mirror the physical café's warmth — making users feel comfortable before they've even ordered.

📱

Mobile-First Layout

Most café customers browse on phones. Large tap targets, thumb-friendly navigation, and single-column menus reduce friction for all age groups.

🗂️

Card-Based Menu

Visual cards with food images, clear names, and prices at a glance reduce cognitive load — users don't need to read paragraphs to decide what they want.

🛒

Minimal Checkout Steps

Reduced the ordering flow to 3 steps: browse → add to cart → confirm. Each unnecessary step is a potential drop-off point, especially for older users.

✍️

Serif Typography

Playfair Display-style fonts convey quality and warmth — the kind of typographic tone you'd expect from a thoughtfully branded café, not a fast food chain.

⭐

Featured Items First

Popular and seasonal items appear at the top — reducing decision fatigue and gently guiding users toward the café's best offerings without overwhelming them.

05 · Key Learnings

What I took away
from this project

01

Constraints spark better design

Working without formal user research data pushed me to think harder about assumptions — questioning every layout choice by asking "would this work for someone who rarely uses a smartphone?"

02

Figma to live site is a full UX story

Taking a project from low-fidelity wireframe all the way to a published Canva website showed me how design decisions shift when real content, images, and interaction replace placeholders.

03

Visual brand is a UX tool, not decoration

Choosing warm colours and typography wasn't just aesthetic — it built trust and comfort, which directly influences whether a user completes an order or abandons the site.

04

Next iteration: real user testing

The biggest gap in this project was the absence of usability testing. My next version would include 5-user tests with varied age groups to validate assumptions and iterate on the checkout flow.
